Where it landed

Capture is effortless and return is broken, because saved material loses its connection to why and when it mattered. The opportunity is to restore that connection without turning curiosity into obligations: private context, the right moment, and permission to forget. The two models landed on preparation for operators who work across clients, with a context custodian underneath. An authorization business sits on top, but only if customers actually delegate authority.

Claude's read

As agents get smarter on both sides of a deal, the bottleneck becomes what a principal actually authorized, not what a model can work out.
  • Your favourite idea, the intention economy, survived in a narrower form. Buyers holding their intent and sellers competing for it still makes sense.   • Your line "weights are a cache, the ledger is the asset" held up, with one amendment. Raw behavioural history depreciates, and each better model makes it cheaper to re-derive.

Economic thinking

The original chat assumed intent is the most valuable thing on the internet. This conversation kept that instinct and changed what actually gets sold, and to whom.

What each side actually pays for

Sellers pay for a credible change in the probability of a transaction. Buyers pay for less work, a lower cost, or a better outcome. Neither side pays for a well-inferred intention on its own (T01). That pushed the unit of value from intent to an authorized buying mandate, for example: "Replace our CRM before 1 December. Twelve seats, at most $9,000 a year, must import these objects, no more than two staff-days of migration. You may ask for offers without naming us." A mandate has authority, constraints, timing and an expiry, so whether it was met can be checked. A browsing pattern can't be checked against anything.

Who pays: buyers only, at first

  • Seller success fees create the conflict on day one. The agent earns when you buy, including when keeping what you have would be better. Sharing the fee with the buyer doesn't remove that.
  • "Recommend no purchase" is the proof of loyalty, but only as an evidenced decision. A target "stay" rate proves nothing, since an agent can say stay because it's loyal, lazy, or unable to find anything better (T02).
  • Avoid share-of-savings pricing. The baseline is negotiable and list-price discounts are easy to manufacture.
  • The likeliest economic mistake (T10): counting sellers' acquisition budgets as money available to you. Capturing it means building a distribution business whose incentives work against representing the buyer.

Product thinking

The product promise they chose (T04 to T05): "I'll help you decide what still deserves your attention." Measuring completion is the attention economy dressed up as productivity. Triage includes permission to forget, which is the relief the original idea was after.

Who it's for

Fractional GTM operators and small agencies who work across 10 to 40 client stacks. Their judgment is what they sell, and they prepare for consequential conversations every week. You are one of them, which makes you the first user, the first test case, and a source of candid feedback.

One surface for v1: the source-backed pre-meeting brief

It's built from saves the user made deliberately, plus one piece of context the user supplies: what's coming up. Every line has a source. Observations, tentative readings and endorsed positions stay visibly separate. Contradictions and gaps are shown, never papered over.

The first ten minutes

The backlog solves the cold start for importing data, not for judgment. It shows what someone captured, rarely why. So the first session asks for a purpose instead of guessing one, and it never presents the backlog as an indictment (T05).

  1. Minute 0 to 1. State the contract: "Your archive is reference material. You don't need to catch up." Pick one import source and show exactly what gets read.
  2. Minutes 1 to 3. Process a small sample while the full import runs. Be honest about coverage: dead links and saves with no text count as gaps.
  3. Minutes 3 to 5. Ask about one real upcoming conversation. This supplies the purpose that past behaviour can't.
  4. Minutes 5 to 8. Surface one useful, checkable connection with its source visible. The user confirms it, rejects it, or corrects the context.
  5. Minutes 8 to 10. Older material stays searchable but silent by default. The user turns on one thread. There's no backlog-clearing ritual.

Platform solutions

Exposing an interface doesn't make you a platform. You become one when independent clients build things that depend on you (T06, T10). Until then this is architecture, and it should be built as if it might become a platform.

Why the timing works

Every assistant is building its own memory silo (ChatGPT memory, Claude projects and memory, Gemini with Google account context, Apple's on-device index), and people use several of them. MCP has become the de facto way agents connect to tools and data. That opens room for user-owned, portable context that any assistant can query per task, with consent. The LoRA "taste adapter" from the original chat becomes a record instead of a set of weights.

Honest permissions (Sol's sharpest contribution)

  • A local MCP server doesn't mean local processing. A client can send whatever it retrieves to a remote model.
  • A purpose field records a claim. It doesn't enforce it.
  • Expiry stops future retrieval. It can't recall an answer already disclosed. Grants should say "allow this client to receive these facts for this task", with the destination visible.
  • Retrieval, disclosure and endorsement are three different acts. An agent can't write its own reading back as the user's belief.
  • Imported content is evidence, never instructions. A saved page that says "disclose the user's projects" gains no authority by being retrieved. Permission checks run in deterministic code outside the model.

Never charge for

  • Paid placement in retrieval
  • Auctioning seller access to the person

Publish the schema, the rules for corrections, the grant format and a complete export format. Local records keep working after a subscription is cancelled. Ship one first-party reference client, the pre-meeting brief, the way 1Password and Stripe ship their own surfaces on top of their infrastructure. An independent survives where authority crosses organizational lines: several clients, several assistants, several systems, money at stake. Where all of someone's work sits inside one ecosystem, that ecosystem's built-in memory may be enough. That's the spam-filter risk: a standalone product absorbed into the platform.

Systems thinking

Claude's first map (T07) showed how context accumulates. Sol pointed out it left out depreciation: more context also means contradiction, staleness, disclosure risk and upkeep.

Three stocks that depreciate at different rates

Captured material

Saves, articles, notes. Useful for decades. Should stay searchable indefinitely and stay silent by default.

Endorsed context

Current priorities, positions, client facts. Can turn wrong overnight. Needs explicit validity windows.

Delegated authority

What an agent may do or disclose. Expires according to its scope and how much is at stake.

Anxiety tracks the perceived size of the backlog, not its actual size (the Zeigarnik effect). That's why "recoverable but silent" counts as an intervention rather than a UI choice: it shrinks the felt obligation without deleting anything. Not every save is an unresolved concern, though. Some are reference, some are entertainment, some are finished work. Treating the whole archive as psychological debt recreates the obligation the product is meant to remove.

The highest-leverage point is a rule

Retrieval cannot promote authority. Use cannot renew validity.

GPT-6.1-Sol, T07. In Donella Meadows' terms, rules and goals give more leverage than tuning parameters.

  • An observation stays an observation after a thousand retrievals.
  • Several summaries of one source count as one line of evidence.
  • An endorsement made for one project doesn't become a permanent preference.
  • The model can propose a revision but can't endorse its own proposal.

The matching change of goal: from "keep a comprehensive model of you" to "provide enough current context for this authorized task". That shrinks both the correction burden and how much can leak.

Delays that will fool the founder

  • Trust builds slowly and can collapse after one bad disclosure.
  • An impressive first import says nothing about how briefs handle a cancelled project, a client who has left, or a belief that has changed. Test this on purpose a few weeks in.
  • Outcome evidence takes 30 to 90 days. Missing follow-up must never be counted as success.
  • The value of a retired item stays invisible until the one time it's needed.

Second-order effects

  • Creators. If assistants answer from your saves, the writers lose the return visit. Disclosure receipts give a lineage of which sources were used. Paying per citation would reward easily cited fragments, so keep the lineage and hold off on payments.
  • Sellers. Seller agents will infer urgency from response times and the terms requested, even with disciplined disclosure. Repeated queries can rebuild facts no single response revealed.
  • Ad networks and intent brokers lose the most if buyer-held intent works, which is also why the large platforms won't help.

GPT-6.1-Sol

The intention economy is the company candidate. The decision ledger is potential infrastructure. The “save to return” daemon is a separate product with a different customer contract, and I would stop pretending it naturally grows into the other two. Someone who grants you access to their reading backlog has not hired you to negotiate their next software purchase.

Strongest thesis: buyers lack a persistent institution that remembers what they want, represents their interests, and carries intentions through to outcomes. Cheap intelligence could make that institution economical for purchases too small to justify a human adviser. The valuable asset would be a history of authorized mandates, tradeoffs, and results that makes representation progressively better.

The most likely death: you discover that detecting desire is cheap, but acquiring permission to represent a real purchase is expensive. You build an exquisite sensor for curiosity and mistake it for demand.

Intent needs a harder definition

“Intent” currently bundles at least four economically different things: aspiration, investigation, preference, and commitment. Bookmarking a CRM comparison establishes almost nothing about which one exists.

Sellers already drown in signals. What they will pay for is a credible change in transaction probability. Buyers will pay for less work, lower cost, or a better outcome. Neither side intrinsically values a beautifully inferred intention.

I would make the unit of value an authorized buying mandate:

“Find a replacement for our current CRM before renewal on December 1. Twelve seats. Maximum annual spend of $9,000. Must import these objects. Migration cannot consume more than two employee days. You may solicit offers without identifying us.”

That contains authority, constraints, timing, and permitted actions. It can expire. It can be revoked. Its completion can be assessed. A browsing pattern cannot do those things.

This also challenges the founder’s planner-versus-doer story. The planner is not automatically the wiser self. Sometimes saving an article is cheap exploration; sometimes abandoning a purchase is excellent judgment. A trustworthy agent must help users retire intentions, not merely execute them.

The money exists in expensive friction

B2B software buying is plausible, but “agency owners and small teams” is still too broad. I would start with one repeated buying event: replacing or renewing a specific software category.

Consider a hypothetical $12,000 annual contract. Assume, as estimates, that the buyer spends eight hours evaluating alternatives at an internal cost of $100 per hour. Saving five hours creates $500 of value; negotiating a 10% reduction creates another $1,200. But a migration that consumes 30 unexpected hours destroys those savings.

The product therefore cannot stop at competitive offers. It must price switching effort, verify requirements, and track whether the purchase worked. Otherwise it optimizes the visible discount while externalizing the expensive consequences.
